Our Approach

How BDI raises money from institutional donors.

Institutional funding requires more than a good project idea. It requires a credible organization, a proven track record, compliance-ready systems, and the ability to co-design programs that meet a donor's strategic priorities without losing sight of community needs. BDI has spent its first three years building exactly that foundation.

Our strategy is not to chase every grant available. It is to build deep, multi-year relationships with a focused group of institutions whose funding priorities align with BDI's thematic areas - and to demonstrate, through every project we implement, that BDI delivers what it promises.

1

Targeted Donor Mapping and Relationship Building

BDI maintains an active donor landscape analysis across all eight program areas, identifying which UN agencies, bilateral funds, foundations, and INGOs are funding work in Liberia and West Africa. We invest in relationship-building before proposals are ever submitted.

2

Consortium and Co-Implementation Models

BDI actively pursues consortium arrangements with larger INGOs and national institutions - as demonstrated by our partnership with Elitrust Finecon on the NaFAA/OPEC Fund fisheries program. This model multiplies our access to larger grants while sharing fiduciary risk.

3

A Fundable Concept Note Bank

BDI maintains a bank of 32 ready-to-deploy concept notes across all eight program areas. When donor calls open, we are not starting from scratch. We are adapting polished, evidence-grounded documents - saving time and sharpening our competitive advantage.

4

Compliance and Governance Investment

Institutional donors require organizational credibility before they fund programs. BDI has proactively invested in the systems that donors demand: PSEA policies, Financial Management Policies, a MEAL Framework, audit readiness, and a five-year Strategic Plan - so we never lose a grant over a compliance gap.

Funding Share Analysis

Institutional donors are BDI's primary engine — now and through 2029.

Since BDI was formally established in 2022, approximately 70% of all funds raised have come from institutional sources — including the completed GEF/Conservation International SBCRP project, which established BDI's environmental track record in Sinoe County.

By 2029, BDI projects that institutional funding will represent approximately 65% of our $2 million annual budget, equivalent to $1.3 million per year. This slight reduction from 70% reflects a deliberate strategy to diversify our income base, with partnerships and individual giving growing to reduce over-dependence on any single funding channel.

The Role Institutional Donors Play

What institutional funding makes possible that nothing else can.

Institutional donors are not just funders. They are co-creators of scale. The financial volume, multi-year structure, and technical requirements they bring enable BDI to build the kind of organizational infrastructure and programmatic depth that truly changes communities - not just for a project cycle, but permanently.

Funding at the Scale Liberia Needs

Community problems in Liberia are not $5,000 problems. They require multi-year, multi-county programs with consistent staffing, infrastructure, and evidence systems. Institutional grants — typically ranging from $50,000 to $500,000+ — are the only funding source capable of operating at that scale. BDI's $2M target by 2029 cannot be reached without institutional funding as its primary pillar.

Legitimacy and Credibility Building

When UNICEF, GEF, or the OPEC Fund trusts BDI with their resources, it signals to every other donor, government partner, and community leader that BDI is an organization worth betting on. Each completed institutional grant makes the next one more likely. Institutional funding is BDI's most powerful credibility tool.

Driving Organizational Quality and Systems

Institutional donors require PSEA compliance, financial audits, logical frameworks, MEAL systems, and transparent reporting. These requirements, while demanding, push BDI to build the institutional quality that distinguishes credible organizations from weak ones. In this way, every institutional grant makes BDI a stronger organization — not just a better-funded one.

Opening Doors to the Global Development Architecture

Institutional relationships connect BDI to the wider global development system — to other implementing partners, to government ministries, to technical assistance networks, and to the policy conversations where Liberia's development future is shaped. This connectivity is a form of value that no financial amount can fully capture.

What We Look for in Institutional Partners

BDI is selective — and proud of it.

Not every grant is the right grant. BDI evaluates institutional funding opportunities against six criteria to ensure that the partnerships we pursue strengthen, rather than distort, our mission and our communities.

Thematic Alignment

The funding must address one of BDI's eight program areas and must be grounded in an evidence-based understanding of Liberia's community development needs. BDI does not design programs around funding. We find funding for programs communities need.

Community Ownership

The program design must allow BDI to conduct genuine community consultation and co-design. BDI will not implement top-down programs that position communities as passive beneficiaries. Institutional donors who share this value are the ones BDI wants to grow with.

Reasonable Compliance Requirements

BDI welcomes rigorous compliance — it makes us stronger. But compliance requirements must be proportionate to the grant size and realistic for a mid-sized Liberian NGO. We are transparent about our current capacity and will not over-promise what we cannot deliver.

Long-Term Relationship Potential

BDI prioritizes institutional relationships with long-term potential over one-off grants. A $50,000 pilot with a donor who is genuinely interested in a $500,000 follow-on is worth more to BDI's mission than a single large grant with no renewal pathway.

Flexible and Adaptive Reporting

Development in Liberia is complex and often unpredictable. BDI values institutional partners who understand that adaptive management — adjusting activities in response to what the evidence shows — is a sign of a good program, not a weak one.

Timely and Predictable Disbursement

Community-facing programs depend on reliable cash flow to pay field staff, purchase supplies, and maintain community trust. BDI prioritizes donors with predictable disbursement schedules and efficient contracting processes that do not leave communities waiting.
