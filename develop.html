Women and youth are not beneficiaries. They are the drivers of Liberia's future.

Women and youth together make up more than 60% of Liberia's population. Yet structural inequalities continue to lock them out of economic opportunity, political participation, and dignified livelihoods. Youth unemployment is high, worsened by insufficient vocational pathways and a challenging investment climate.
     Women face gender-based violence at alarming rates, bear the brunt of unpaid care work, and encounter persistent bias when accessing finance, land, and leadership roles. BDI's Gender and Youth Empowerment program is not a peripheral add-on to our work – it is a core driver of everything we do.
      Through entrepreneurship training, microfinance facilitation, youth leadership development, and deliberate norm-shifting at the community level, we are working to dismantle the structural barriers that have held women and youth back for too long.

570 kilometers of coastline. A blue economy waiting to be built.

Liberia’s coastline and inland waterways represent one of the country’s most underutilized economic and food security assets. Fish is the most widely consumed source of animal protein in Liberia, and artisanal fishing is a primary livelihood for thousands of coastal families.

Yet the sector is constrained by overfishing, poor post-harvest infrastructure, limited market access, and inadequate technical capacity. Women play a central, often invisible role in fish processing and trade – yet they remain largely excluded from formal support systems and market opportunities.

BDI’s Fisheries and Aquaculture program is designed to change that. By training fishers, supporting aquaculture enterprises, building market linkages, and centering women and youth as entrepreneurs within the blue economy, we aim to turn an underutilized sector into a sustainable engine of food security, income, and coastal community resilience.

OVERVIEW

Disability is not a barrier to dignity. Exclusion is - and BDI is here to end it.

Persons with disabilities in Liberia face one of the most pervasive and least addressed forms of exclusion in the country's development landscape. They cannot access education, health services, public buildings, or transportation on an equal basis with others, and routinely face discrimination in employment, housing, and civic life.

Years of civil war have left many Liberians with physical and psychological disabilities, while poverty deepens the exclusion of those who cannot afford assistive devices or find schools and employers willing to include them. Liberia has signed the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) – but implementation remains slow, underfunded, and largely unmonitored. BDI's disability inclusion program is built on a simple conviction: our commitment to "leaving no one behind" is meaningless unless it explicitly and systematically includes persons with disabilities. This is not a peripheral concern. It is a core expression of our values.

OUR FOUR DELIVERY PILLARS

A

Direct Services and Assistive Support

Connecting persons with disabilities to wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids, and white canes, alongside rehabilitation referrals and community-based support to enable full participation in economic and social life.

B

Inclusive Education

Supporting primary schools in creating genuinely inclusive learning environments – through teacher training, infrastructure advocacy, and adapted learning materials for children with disabilities.

C

Vocational Training and Economic Inclusion

Designing and delivering vocational programs adapted to the specific needs and abilities of PWDs, creating pathways into agriculture, small business, digital work, and craft-based enterprise.

D

Advocacy and Policy Engagement

Engaging government and the legislature to advocate for full CRPD implementation – through policy briefs, national consultations, and amplifying the voices of persons with disabilities in policy spaces.

Key Objectives

Provide assistive devices and rehabilitation support to PWDs in target communities.

Support 15 schools annually with inclusive education infrastructure and training.

Deliver vocational training and employment pathways tailored for PWDs.

Mainstream disability inclusion across 100% of BDI programs by 2027.

Grounded in International Law, Driven by Local Reality

Liberia has ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and enacted domestic disability legislation. Yet implementation remains slow and largely unmonitored. BDI's role is to bridge that gap – documenting rights violations, advocating for enforcement, providing direct services, and shifting the social norms that treat disability as a source of shame rather than a call to action.

OVERVIEW

No program survives without evidence. No evidence scales without partnerships. No partnership thrives without trust.

No organization achieves lasting development impact in isolation. BDI's ability to serve communities at scale depends on three things working together: the strength of our partnerships, the quality of our evidence, and the diversity of our funding. This program area is the institutional engine that makes all other programs possible. BDI occupies a unique position in Liberia's civil society landscape – locally led, credible, corruption-resistant, and deeply mission-driven. These qualities make us a natural partner for international NGOs, foundations, bilateral donors, and government agencies seeking to maximize the impact of their investment in Liberia. We do not chase funding. We build relationships, generate evidence, and grow partnerships that outlast any single project cycle.
