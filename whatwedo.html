OVERVIEW

From survival farming to a sustainable agribusiness economy.

Agriculture is the backbone of Liberia's economy and the primary livelihood for approximately 70% of the workforce. Yet too many smallholder farmers remain trapped in subsistence-level cycles, locally described as "hand to mouth" – limited by inadequate market access, climate vulnerability, and a lack of financing options.

BDI's Agriculture and Agribusiness program is designed to change that narrative, transforming farming from a survival strategy into a commercially viable, dignified, and climate-resilient enterprise. We work across the full agricultural value chain – from production and processing to commercialization – integrating climate-smart practices, cooperative strengthening, and market linkages that build lasting rural prosperity.

OVERVIEW

Where healthcare is a luxury, we make it a right.

Liberia's health system is under severe strain. With fewer than 51 doctors serving a population of over 5 million, and maternal mortality among the highest in Africa, the burden of preventable illness and death falls most heavily on rural women, children, and the poor. High out-of-pocket costs push vulnerable families deeper into poverty with every hospital visit, while menstrual health remains a taboo subject in many communities — causing girls to miss school and women to face avoidable health consequences. BDI's Health and Nutrition program addresses these interconnected gaps through direct service expansion, health education, preventive campaigns, and evidence-based policy advocacy. We do not wait for communities to come to us. We bring health care to where people live.

OVERVIEW

Education is not a privilege. In Liberia, we are fighting to make it a reality.

In Liberia, where 15 to 20% of school-age children remain out of school and the average Liberian completes fewer than five years of schooling, the education deficit is not just a statistic. It is a cycle, passing poverty from one generation to the next. Girls face the highest barriers – from the cost of fees to the absence of menstrual hygiene support that keeps them home for days every month. Teachers are underprepared, schools are under-resourced, and the digital literacy skills that increasingly determine economic participation are virtually absent in rural communities. BDI's Education program tackles access, quality, and relevance together – ensuring more children are in school, learning effectively, and graduating with the skills that today's economy actually needs.

Overview

Liberia's forests are a global treasure. We intend to keep them that way.

Liberia holds one of West Africa's most significant remaining rainforest reserves... BDI's Environment and Climate Resilience program treats environmental protection not as a standalone sector, but as the foundation on which all sustainable development rests. Through community-led conservation, clean energy access, and advocacy for land rights, we are building communities that can face climate change and thrive.

Key Objectives

Strengthen community-based natural resource management.

Expand access to clean energy and clean cooking solutions.

Conduct waste management and sanitation awareness campaigns.

Ensure women and youth lead environmental stewardship.

Our Approach

  • Facilitate community NRM committees with clear governance structures for sustainable land management.
  • Introduce solar-powered solutions and clean cookstoves in rural and peri-urban households.
  • Run conservation awareness campaigns directly linked to agricultural and fisheries practices.
  • Advocate for community land rights and responsible concession management.
  • Integrate climate adaptation strategies across all BDI agricultural and fisheries programs.
