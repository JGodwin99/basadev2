Overview

From Survival Farming To A Sustainable Agribusiness Economy.

Agriculture remains the backbone of Liberia’s economy, employing an estimated 65–70% of the population and contributing roughly one-third of the national GDP. Yet the sector continues to underperform relative to its potential. Most smallholder farmers operate on less than two hectares of land, rely on rain-fed production, and face persistent constraints including low productivity, post-harvest losses that can exceed 30–40% for perishable crops, weak aggregation systems, limited access to finance, and highly fragmented markets. In many rural communities, farming is still largely a subsistence activity, often described locally as “hand to mouth,” with minimal surplus for trade or reinvestment.
BASA Development Initiatives’ Agriculture and Agribusiness Program is designed to shift this reality in a practical and sustained way. At its core is a deliberate transition from subsistence farming to farming as a business. BDI applies an integrated production, processing, and commercialization system that supports farmers and producer groups to move beyond primary production into value addition and structured market engagement. This approach recognizes that productivity gains alone are not enough; farmers must be linked to functioning markets, equipped to meet quality standards, and positioned to capture greater value along the chain.
On the production side, BDI promotes climate-smart and market-oriented agriculture, including improved seed systems, soil fertility management, climate-resilient practices, and basic mechanization where feasible. This is combined with extension support that is responsive to both agronomic and business needs. At the processing level, the program supports small and medium agro-enterprises and cooperatives to reduce post-harvest losses, improve product quality, and meet market specifications through appropriate technologies and skills development. On commercialization, BDI focuses on aggregation, market linkages, and enterprise development, helping farmers and groups to negotiate better prices, access structured buyers, and participate in local and regional value chains. The program also invests in strengthening farmer organizations and cooperatives as viable business entities. This includes governance, financial management, and business planning, alongside access to savings and financing mechanisms such as VSLAs and linkages to formal financial institutions. By improving coordination and scale, these structures enable farmers to move from isolated production to collective market participation.
Through this integrated model, BDI is not only improving yields but also increasing incomes, reducing vulnerability to climate and market shocks, and creating pathways for rural employment, particularly for women and youth. The overall aim is to reposition agriculture in Liberia and Africa from a survival strategy to a competitive and resilient agribusiness sector that contributes meaningfully to food security, economic growth, and rural transformation.

Key Objectives

Empower women and youth to actively participate in and lead agribusiness through skills development, enterprise support, and access to finance.

Increase agricultural productivity and resilience by promoting climate-smart, market- oriented farming practices.

Expand access to quality seeds, tools, and appropriate technologies across production and post-harvest stages.

Strengthen integrated value chains by linking production, processing, and commercialization through competitive farmer organizations and agribusinesses.
