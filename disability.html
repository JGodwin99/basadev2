Overview

Disability is not a barrier to dignity. Exclusion is - and BDI is here to end it.

Persons with disabilities in Liberia face one of the most pervasive and least addressed forms of exclusion in the country's development landscape. They cannot access education, health services, public buildings, or transportation on an equal basis with others, and routinely face discrimination in employment, housing, and civic life. Years of civil war have left many Liberians with physical and psychological disabilities, while poverty deepens the exclusion of those who cannot afford assistive devices or find schools and employers willing to include them. Liberia has signed the United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) - but implementation remains slow, underfunded, and largely unmonitored. BDI's disability inclusion program is built on a simple conviction: our commitment to "leaving no one behind" is meaningless unless it explicitly and systematically includes persons with disabilities. This is not a peripheral concern. It is a core expression of our values.

Our Four Delivery Pillars

A

Direct Services and Assistive Support

Connecting persons with disabilities to wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids, and white canes, alongside rehabilitation referrals and community-based support to enable full participation in economic and social life.

B

Inclusive Education

Supporting primary schools in creating genuinely inclusive learning environments - through teacher training, infrastructure advocacy, and adapted learning materials for children with disabilities.

C

Vocational Training and Economic Inclusion

Designing and delivering vocational programs adapted to the specific needs and abilities of PWDs, creating pathways into agriculture, small business, digital work, and craft-based enterprise.

D

Advocacy and Policy Engagement

Engaging government and the legislature to advocate for full CRPD implementation - through policy briefs, national consultations, and amplifying the voices of persons with disabilities in policy spaces.

Key Objectives

Provide assistive devices and rehabilitation support to PWDs in target communities.

Support 15 schools annually with inclusive education infrastructure and training.

Deliver vocational training and employment pathways tailored for PWDs.

Mainstream disability inclusion across 100% of BDI programs by 2027.

Grounded in International Law, Driven by Local Reality

Liberia has ratified the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and enacted domestic disability legislation. Yet implementation remains slow and largely unmonitored. BDI's role is to bridge that gap - documenting rights violations, advocating for enforcement, providing direct services, and shifting the social norms that still treat disability as a source of shame rather than a call to action.

Be a Founding Partner

BDI's Disability Inclusion program is in its founding phase. No active project exists yet - which means your support now shapes what this program becomes. Here are four ways to be part of it from the ground up.

Program Roadmap

2025 - 2026

Establish partnerships with disability-focused organizations. Submit first funding proposals. Begin community scoping in Sinoe and Montserrado Counties.

2026 - 2027

Launch first assistive device distribution and vocational training pilot. Begin inclusive education support in 15 target schools. Produce first CRPD policy brief.

2027 - 2029

Scale to reach 300 PWDs annually with assistive devices. Mainstream disability inclusion across all 8 BDI programs. Sustain policy advocacy at national level.

Partner with us to build a Liberia where no person with a disability is left behind.
